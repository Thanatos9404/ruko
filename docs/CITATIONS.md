# Ruko: citation audit

Audited on 17 October 2026. In scope: the landing page (`src/pages/landing/sections.tsx`), `README.md`, `shared/sources.ts`, the truth lines in `shared/patterns.ts` (current wording on 17 Oct, after the edit that shortened them), `docs/RESEARCH.md`, `docs/BUSINESS.md` and `docs/MEMO.md`.

**Method.** Each URL was fetched with curl and the HTML or PDF converted to text and searched. Sites that block scripts (NPCI, ThePrint, cybercrime.gov.in) were opened in a browser. UK Finance and Pay.UK PDFs were read through WebFetch. Quotes are copied from the source text, not from our docs. Every row was accessed on **17 Oct 2026** unless it says otherwise.

**Status key**

- **Verified.** The link resolves and the number or wording matches the source.
- **Partially verified.** The link resolves and the core fact is there, but our wording overstates it, changes its scope, or adds something the source does not say.
- **Not verified.** The source could not be reached, or the claim is not in the cited source.

## 3. Follow-up checks (a) to (g)

**(a) Which headline figure: ₹22,845 crore (2024) or ₹22,931 crore (2025)?**

| | ₹22,845.73 crore | ₹22,931 crore |
|---|---|---|
| Source | MHA, Lok Sabha USQ 344, 22 Jul 2025 (primary, parliamentary answer) | RBI Discussion Paper, 9 Apr 2026, para xi table (primary, but it reproduces NCRP data) |
| Period | Calendar year 2024 (the answer says "in the year 2023 and 2024") | "Year 2025". Not labelled calendar or FY, but the table's 2023 row (13.1 lakh, ₹7,465 crore) matches MHA's calendar-2023 figures exactly, so it is almost certainly calendar years. |
| Definition | "Amount Reported" by victims on NCRP and CFCFRMS for 19,18,865 financial-fraud complaints. Victim-reported, not bank-confirmed; includes money later frozen or recovered. | "Figures from ... NCRP" on "frauds related to digital payments". Also victim-reported to NCRP, **not** bank-reported frauds. |
| Cross-check | The RBI table gives 2024 as ₹22,848 crore, within 0.01%. | ThePrint (1 Feb 2026, citing MHA data) gives 2025 as "at least Rs 22,495 crore", about 2% lower. The RBI's 2024 count (24 lakh) also does not match MHA's 19,18,865 complaints, so the count basis differs. |

Neither figure is bank-reported. RBI bank-fraud statistics are a separate, financial-year series. Since the July 2024 fraud directions, banks report only payment frauds "concluded as fraud committed on bank(s)" (RBI Trend and Progress, table note 7), so they understate push-payment scams.

**Recommendation.** Use **₹22,845 crore (2024, MHA)** as the one headline everywhere. It is an exact parliamentary figure with a clear definition, and two official sources agree on it. The 2025 value is disputed (₹22,931 vs ₹22,495 crore). Phrasing: "In 2024, Indians reported ₹22,845 crore lost to cyber fraud on the national cybercrime portal (MHA, Lok Sabha, 22 Jul 2025)." If a 2025 line is wanted, add "and about as much again in 2025 (₹22,931 crore, NCRP data cited by the RBI)". Change the memo (item 6).

**(b) "RBI liability directions take effect on 1 January 2027 … treat ignoring a bank's clear warning as customer negligence" (MEMO).** **Keep.** Verified (rows 4a to 4c): the directions apply to transactions "on or after January 1, 2027", and 4(20C)(iii) lists "not paying attention to specific, directed and clear warnings from the bank that a prospective transaction is likely a scam" as customer negligence. Optional tightening: quote the clause exactly.

**(c) "RBI's April 2026 paper proposes a payment delay and a 'trusted person'" (MEMO).** **Soften** (item 8). The paper "sets out the following four options" and asks for comments; the one-hour lag and trusted person are two of them. BUSINESS's "floats" is accurate.

**(d) TRAI, AI-voice declaration and 1600xx.** **Soften** (items 9, 14, 15).

- **PR 119/2026 is final, not a consultation.** It announces the TCCCPR (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2026, "introduced ... today" on 18 Sep 2026. The draft went out for consultation on 13 Mar 2026. The pre-declaration duty is mandatory ("It has been mandated") for all A2P calls, including artificial voice. The release gives no commencement date.
- **1600xx is mandatory for banks.** The RBI circular RBI/2024-25/105 (17 Jan 2025, verified) tells regulated entities to "Undertake transactional / service calls only using '1600xx' numbering series, when operationalized". A TRAI direction reported by PTI on 19 Nov 2025 then set deadlines: commercial banks "by January 1, 2026"; large NBFCs, payments banks and SFBs by 1 Feb 2026; other NBFCs, co-operative banks and RRBs by 1 Mar 2026. We read this in Outlook Business, not the TRAI direction itself.
- **What protects 1600xx calls.** PR 119/2026 says the 1600xx and 1601xx series "shall not be flagged as suspected spam for recipients", and bars call-management apps from "blanket blocking, filtering, or spam tagging" them. Individual users "retain full freedom to block".

**(e) "Bolna … ₹5.52 a minute".** **Soften** (item 10). Bolna's preferred-models page shows "$0.06/min (₹5.52/min) at standard wallet tiers", which implies **₹92/$**, the rate BUSINESS uses. It covers speech-to-text, LLM and text-to-speech only; telephony ("rounded to minutes") and a "flat per-minute" platform fee are extra (call-pricing page). Inc42 independently reports self-serve Bolna customers pay "around ₹5.5 per minute".

**(f) "Convin claims outbound fraud calls".** **Keep.** Convin's own blog (https://convin.ai/blog/fraud-call-prevent-bfsi, HTTP 200) has a section titled "Outbound Automation: Taking Proactive Action Against Fraud Callers". It describes contacting "at-risk users as soon as fraud call triggers occur", plus account-freeze workflows. Its demo agent offers "English, Hindi, Hinglish". These are vendor claims and are labelled as such in RESEARCH.

**(g) MuleHunter.AI.** **Keep.** The RBI discussion paper (primary) says "In 2024, Reserve Bank's wholly owned subsidiary - Reserve Bank Innovation Hub (RBIH) has built Mulehunter.AI to enable quick and effective detection of mule bank accounts by the banks". TaxGuru (18 Mar 2026), reporting a Lok Sabha reply, says it is "currently operational in 26 banks" (secondary). It flags mule accounts rather than scoring each payment. The memo's "BioCatch and MuleHunter.AI score payments but don't talk" is slightly loose. Better: "BioCatch and RBIH's MuleHunter.AI flag risky sessions and mule accounts, but don't talk to the customer."
