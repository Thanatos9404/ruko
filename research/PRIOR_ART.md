# Ruko: prior art

Compiled 2026-10-17. Sections: A products and public programmes, B open-source repositories (machine-readable list in [`prior_art_repos.json`](prior_art_repos.json)), C academic work and datasets, D gap analysis.

Conventions: every product row links a page opened during this research unless marked **[snippet only]** (only a search-result snippet was seen). Numbers are reported only as stated by the vendor, regulator, authors or press, with the source named; vendor numbers are self-reported. Nothing is estimated. Read-only browsing; no logins.

Limitations: the web-search budget for this run ran out at the start of section C, so section C relied on known arXiv IDs (each opened and checked), repository citations and Hugging Face cards; some arXiv versions and some prior art (e.g. WhatsApp fact-check tiplines, Indian bank IVR confirmation calls) may be missing. Several publisher/vendor pages returned 403 (Mastercard, Finextra, MDPI, IEEE, Revolut); secondary reports were used instead and are linked.

**Bottom line:** every individual Ruko element has prior art (coaching detection: BioCatch, Feedzai, Revolut, Second Voice, CoachGuard; payment hold with questions: Westpac SaferPay; trusted contact: Monzo, RBI April 2026 proposal; explainable TF-IDF+LR scam classifier: spam-scan, Sentinel). The combination - bank-side hold -> outbound AI phone call in Indian languages -> coaching interview -> naming the scam with a cited Indian official statement -> optional call to a trusted contact - was not found in any examined product, repo or paper. Others are ahead on on-device privacy, scale and real data, carrier/registry blocking, payee/mule intelligence and published outcomes.

### A4. Notes

- **Closest commercial analogues to Ruko's loop** are bank-side: Westpac SaferPay (questions before a flagged payment, then block), Revolut's intervention flow (asks whether someone is guiding you, then a human chat), and Feedzai/BioCatch (active-call signal + payment delay). All of these are **in-app text flows or human agents**, not an outbound AI voice call. None of the pages opened describes one in an Indian language.
- **Trusted contact as a payment control already exists**: Monzo Trusted Contacts (since Jul 2024) holds payments above a limit until a chosen person approves, and the RBI's April 2026 discussion paper proposes trusted-person authentication for customers 70+ above Rs 50,000. Ruko's trusted-contact *phone call* is a variant of this, not a new idea.
- **"Safe phrase"** (Starling) is awareness advice, not a product; Ruko's "official truth sentence" is a different idea (naming the scam at the moment of payment), but both rely on a pre-agreed or authoritative script.
- **India's payment-time defences are list-based**: FRI (risky mobile numbers), Suspect Registry (identifiers), MuleHunter/Bureau/Signzy (mule accounts). They act on *who* is being paid, and PhonePe/Paytm/GPay surface them as declines or warnings. None of the Indian pages opened describes a conversation with the customer about *why* they are paying.
- **Device-side Indian coverage is thin**: Google's live-call detection in India is Pixel 9+ only; Truecaller's AI scanner was US/English-only; Samsung's voice-phishing alert is Korea-only.

### B4. Observations

- Most Indian repos are 2025-2026 hackathon or student projects with 0-8 stars; none reports deployment with a bank or real customer traffic. Results quoted in READMEs (e.g. Prahari's 136-case evaluation) are self-reported on author-built data.
- 12 of the 27 India-focused repos ship no licence, so their code and data cannot be copied; their ideas can still be cited.
- Reusable Indian **data on GitHub** is thin: junioralive (MIT, ~2,000 spam/ham SMS, mostly promotional spam), skyaara (CC BY 4.0, 172 genuine transaction SMS usable as hard negatives) and the Tanglish corpus (CC BY 4.0 per README, 754 constructed scam SMS). There is no openly licensed Indian *scam call transcript* corpus on GitHub in this set; the openly licensed call/dialogue corpora are English (BothBosu) or Mandarin (TeleAntiFraud-28k, gated). Hugging Face has more Indian text datasets - see section C2.
- Two repos independently report the false-positive trap Ruko's text line must avoid: classifiers trained on public (Western) spam flag genuine Indian bank debit alerts and OTP messages (VivekVagale/scam-shield, Het161/DHANRAKSHAK).
- Closest functional prior art to Ruko's loop is not Indian: `adwik1401/second-voice` (UK, MIT, Sep 2026 hackathon) already does 'risky transfer -> voice check -> listen for coaching -> release/pause/escalate'. Ruko should cite it and differentiate on outbound telephony, Indian languages, trusted-contact call and the cited Indian pattern library.

### C3. Takeaways

- Openly licensed **scam-call** data is English (BothBosu, synthetic, US scam types) or Mandarin (TeleAntiFraud-28k, gated). The real-call corpora (KorCCVi, TeleAntiFraud's source recordings) came from regulators/operators and are not redistributable.
- Two independent sources (EarShield on Korean calls; ScamGuardBench on SMS) say the dominant failure is **false positives on legitimate look-alikes**, and EarShield shows it specifically for character n-gram TF-IDF without hard negatives. Ruko's evaluation should report false-positive rate on genuine bank/UPI/courier messages and calls, not only accuracy.
- Real-time vishing work (Shen et al. 2024, 2025; SAFE-QAQ 2026; EarShield) studies *listening to the scam call on the victim's side*. I found no paper on a **bank-initiated call to the customer at payment time** that interviews them about coaching; the nearest is the 2019 EMNLP identity-fraud dialogue agent (Wang et al.), which chooses questions to expose a fraudster, not to protect a victim.

| **Scale and real data** | BioCatch: data from 370+ institutions, 19 billion sessions a month ([BioCatch](https://www.biocatch.com/press-release/global-banking-scams-increase-by-35)); Featurespace: >100 billion payment events a year ([Visa](https://investor.visa.com/news/news-details/2024/Visa-Completes-Acquisition-of-Featurespace/default.aspx)); Google Pay: >1 million warnings a week in India ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/20/google-steps-up-ai-scam-protection-in-india-but-gaps-remain)); Pindrop: used by 14 of the top 20 banks ([Pindrop](https://www.pindrop.com/products/pindrop-pulse/)). | Ruko's classifier is trained on synthetic text plus a cited library; it has no labelled Indian call outcomes. ### D3. Regulatory fit and open risks

- **The RBI is exploring the same intervention point.** The April 2026 discussion paper proposes a one-hour lag at the payer's bank for authorised push payments of Rs 10,000 and above, and trusted-person authentication for vulnerable customers ([RBI](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23810)). A Ruko call could run inside such a window. The paper also flags a legal problem: a trusted person has no legal interest in the account ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-safeguard-elderly-rbi-suggests-trusted-person-approval-high-value-digital-payments/)). Ruko's contact should be consulted, not given approval power, unless rules change. No final rule was found.
- **Call authenticity.** The same RBI page notes TRAI's "1600xx" series for transactional/service calls ([RBI](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23810)). Ruko's outbound calls should come from such numbers, and the bank app could confirm the call, as Monzo Call Status and CommBank CallerCheck do ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2024/03/battling-impersonation-scams-monzo-innovative-approach.html), [CommBank](https://www.commbank.com.au/articles/newsroom/2024/08/cba-cuts-scam-losses-for-customers.html)). Sanchar Saathi's beta "Trusted Contact Details" service for verifying official bank numbers is another check ([Sanchar Saathi](https://sancharsaathi.gov.in/)).
- **Reach.** Device-side protections cover few Indians today: Google's live-call detection is Pixel 9+ only, and TechCrunch notes Pixel's under-1% market share ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/20/google-steps-up-ai-scam-protection-in-india-but-gaps-remain)). A bank-side phone call reaches any phone, which is Ruko's strongest argument.

### D4. Honest one-line positioning

Ruko does not invent coaching detection, payment holds, trusted contacts or scam classifiers; all exist (Westpac, Revolut, Feedzai, BioCatch, Monzo, Second Voice, CoachGuard). What I did not find elsewhere is the **combination**: a bank-side hold that triggers an **outbound AI phone call in Indian languages**, which interviews the customer, names the specific scam with a **cited Indian official statement**, and can **phone a trusted contact**, backed by an open, India-first pattern library.
